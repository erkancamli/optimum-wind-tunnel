What is real here

  • The coding. Every shard carries a random coefficient vector over GF(2⁸). Relays recode by mixing what they already hold, without decoding first. The probe runs Gaussian elimination on the actual bytes of your file.
  • The geography. Nodes sit in 38 hosting hubs weighted toward where Ethereum nodes cluster. Latency is great-circle distance × 1.5 at 200 km per ms of fibre.
  • The bandwidth. 20% of nodes at 1 Gbps and 80% at 50 Mbps, the same split the OptimumP2P paper used in its Ethshadow runs.
  • Fairness. Both protocols get the same peers, the same links, the same loss and the same reliable transport. A lost packet costs a retransmit for both. Coding helps because a node never waits for one particular shard.

The rules each side follows

  • Gossipsub: mesh of D = 6. A node must hold the whole block before it forwards it. IDONTWANT cancels queued copies, and IHAVE/IWANT on a 700 ms heartbeat rescues stranded nodes.
  • mump2p: the publisher sends 1.5·k coded shards round-robin, matching the dev defaults. Relays forward fresh recoded shards as soon as they hold any, send IDONTWANT once decoded, and pull with IWANT when stuck.
  • Not modelled: CPU for validation and decoding, QUIC, peer scoring, signature checks and the real mesh. This is a wind tunnel, not a mainnet replay.

Out in the real world

  • mump2p went live on Ethereum mainnet on 20 Oct 2026, launching with 16 operators. Announcement
  • Lido's independent Hoodi test: p95 block arrival went from 1,614 to 1,207 ms, late blocks from 1.97% to 0.34%, and wrong head votes fell 45%. Lido forum
  • The cable scenarios are modelled on real events: the C-Lion1 Baltic cut in Nov 2024 and the Red Sea cuts near Jeddah in Sep 2025. Here a cut corridor drops every link that rides it, which is the worst case.
